Phishing
 = social engineering to trick people 
   into revealing sensitive information 
   or running malware on their devices

 - phishing - cast a wide net, hoping to catch a few victims
 - spear phishing - targeted attack on a specific individual
 - whaling - targeted attack on a high-profile individual (CEO, CFO)

Social engineering 101
 - scarcity  - push the victim to act before they think (FOMO)
 - urgency   - time pressure (eg. within 24 hours, immediately)
 - authority - impersonate a figure of authority for quick compliance
 - fear      - trigger a protective reaction, urgent action (eg. "your account has been compromised")
 - curiosity - promising interesting content (eg. random USB drive, "confidential report", "secret video")
 - trust     - familiar sender/brand/website or colleague (make it seem routine)

Cognitive biases
 - overconfidence bias - "I know better than to fall for phishing" (spoiler: you don't)
 - confirmation bias - "this is what I expected to see" (eg. a fake bank email confirming a recent transaction)
 - authority bias - "this is from my boss, I should do it"

Phishing techniques

URL & domain manipulation 
 - URL masking
    - normal site redirects to a malicious one
    - eg. http://www.google.com -> http://malicious.com

 - Homograph attacks
    - fake website looks like a real one
    - eg. go0gle.com, Linked1n.com

 - Typosquatting
    - register a domain similar to a popular one, hoping for typos
    - eg. amazn.com, facebok.com, tryhacme.com

Email spoofing

 - changing the "From" field in an email to make it look like it's from a trusted source
 - SMTP does not have built-in authentication of email addresses
 - this is mostly mitigated by good mail clients (there will be a big warning text next to the name)

 - using a similar domain to a trusted one
 - eg. support@acme-secure.com instead of support@acme.com

Login cloning
 - create a fake login page that looks like the real one
 - you wouldn't believe how many people enter their credentials
 - there is software for this

Phishing campaign

1) Planning & Scoping
 - who to target and what to measure

2) Reconnaissance
 - use public info, keep within scope

3) Scenario and Pyload development
 - Payloads should support learning, not exploitation (avoid malware)

4) Exploitation and Post-Exploitation
 - run it

5) Reporting and Debriefing
 - Analyse what happened and why (click rates, timing, submission attempts, behaviour)

6) Recommendations table
 - open rate, click rate, credential entry rate, attachment open rate, reporting rate
 - what worked, what didn't, what to improve
 - eg. the 6% credential entry rate is a high risk, so we recommend... (eg. education)
